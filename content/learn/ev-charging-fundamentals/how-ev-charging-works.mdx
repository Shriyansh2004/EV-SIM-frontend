## The charging process

When an EV plugs into a charger, several things happen in sequence — both physically (power flow) and logically (protocol messages).

## Physical layer

1. **Connection** — The EV connects to the charger via a cable and connector (Type 1, Type 2, CCS, CHAdeMO, etc.)
2. **Handshake** — The charger and EV negotiate power limits through the charging protocol (ISO 15118 or basic PWM signaling)
3. **Power delivery** — AC or DC current flows from the grid through the charger to the EV battery
4. **Termination** — Charging stops when the battery reaches target SoC, the user unplugs, or an error occurs

## AC vs DC charging

| Type | Typical power | Use case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AC Level 1 | 1.4–1.9 kW | Home outlets (overnight) |
| AC Level 2 | 3.7–22 kW | Home/workplace/public |
| DC Fast (Level 3) | 50–350+ kW | Highway corridors |

EV-SIM simulates AC charging by default (up to 22 kW) but the OCPP message flow is identical regardless of power level.

## Energy accounting

Chargers measure delivered energy in **kWh** (kilowatt-hours). The CSMS uses meter values to track consumption for billing, load management, and reporting.
