## Charging levels

### Level 1 — AC (120V)

Uses a standard household outlet. Delivers 1.4–1.9 kW. Suitable for overnight charging with 8–12 hour sessions.

### Level 2 — AC (208–240V)

The most common public and workplace charging. Delivers 3.7–22 kW depending on circuit capacity. EV-SIM's default virtual chargers operate at Level 2 power levels.

### Level 3 — DC Fast Charging

Bypasses the EV's onboard AC-DC converter. Delivers 50–350+ kW for rapid highway charging. Requires CCS or CHAdeMO connectors.

## Connector types

| Connector | Region | Charging type | Max power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type 1 (J1772) | North America | AC | 7.4 kW |
| Type 2 (Mennekes) | Europe | AC | 22 kW |
| CCS Combo | Global | AC + DC | 350+ kW |
| CHAdeMO | Japan/legacy | DC | 100 kW |
| NACS (Tesla) | North America | AC + DC | 250+ kW |

<Callout variant="info" title="OCPP abstraction">
OCPP operates above the physical connector layer. The protocol doesn't specify connector types — it manages authorization, sessions, and metering regardless of the plug standard.
</Callout>

## What EV-SIM models

EV-SIM abstracts connector details and focuses on the OCPP protocol layer. When you create a virtual charger, you specify:

- **Max power (kW)** — determines simulated energy delivery rate
- **Connector count** — number of simultaneous charging ports

The OCPP messages (`TransactionEvent`, `MeterValues`, etc.) are identical whether the underlying connector is Type 2 or CCS.
